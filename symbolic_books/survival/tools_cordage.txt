Tools and cordage: a knife, cord, and container solve more camp problems than improvising from scratch. Keep edges covered. Natural cord from bark or roots only comes from plants you are allowed to cut and can identify. This is not a weapons guide.

Use the knife away from your body, on a firm surface, and sheath it when you move. A dull edge slips, so a small stone belongs with the blade. Do not throw it, and do not pry with the only knife you have. A covered edge does not cut the bottle in the pack. This is handling, not a fight.

Carry cord you already trust before you strip a living plant. A few meters can hang a pot clear of flame, tie a shelter ridge, or repair a strap. Learn two knots you can also undo. Wet natural fiber swells and can jam a knot you tied while it was dry.

A needle, a little tape, and cord stop a small tear from becoming a lost strap or a split handle. Brighten a tool handle so it shows against leaves. A lighter in a dry bag is the simple spark; practice it at home. This note does not teach you to burn the country.
