Shelter and fire: get out of wind and wet ground first. A small contained fire needs clear fuel, a safe distance from brush, and a way to put it out. Do not light a fire where local rules forbid it. This is a planning note, not a wilderness course.

Pick the site before the flame. Choose ground that sheds rain, above a flood line, and clear of dead trees that can drop limbs. Face the opening away from the wind. A finger-width of dry insulation under you holds more heat than a tall roof. This is planning, not a building code.

Build a fire only where rules allow it, on scraped mineral soil, with fuel upwind and a way to drown it. Start with tinder and pencil twigs, and add thicker wood after the flame is honest. Feel the ashes cold before you leave. A banned day or a hard wind means no flame.

Without a fire, stay dry, covered, and off the ground, and keep the wind off your back. Any flame inside a closed shelter can foul the air, so leave a vent and do not sleep against a heater. Wet clothes next to the skin steal heat even in mild air. This does not treat a cold injury.
