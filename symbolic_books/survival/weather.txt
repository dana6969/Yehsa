Weather: dark building clouds, sudden wind, and thunder mean you should leave high ground and open water. Cold plus wet clothing steals heat. This note does not forecast a local storm.

Read the sky you have. A hard wind shift, a fast drop in temperature, and a dark towering cloud are reasons to leave ridges and lone tall trees. A shorter gap between flash and thunder only means the cell is closer. Do not wait for a forecast you cannot make. Get lower.

Wind that rises and holds one direction often comes before rain. Face the shelter away from it, and weight anything that can blow away. Blowing dust is a reason to seek a low sturdy spot, not a hilltop. This note is not a warning siren and not a local forecast.

A clear night is often colder than a cloudy one. Put the rain layer on before you are soaked, and keep a hat where you can reach it. Stop early enough to camp in daylight. The plan is to be dry before the wind arrives, not after.

Heat is a travel cost: still air, a high sun, and no shade. Move earlier and later, rest at midday, and cover your head. Pushing on through dizziness is a reason to stop and get help, not a goal. This note does not set a temperature rule.
