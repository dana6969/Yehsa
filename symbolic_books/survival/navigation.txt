Navigation: the sun rises generally east and sets generally west. A map and compass beat guesswork. If you are lost, staying put and making yourself visible is often safer than wandering. This is not a complete orienteering manual.

Walk to a landmark you can name, a boulder, a saddle, or a distinct tree, then pick the next. Count paces on a stretch you already measured so distance is a number. Stop while the last sure point is still clear. Turning around is a plan, not a failure.

A shoreline, a ridge, or a stream can be a handrail you keep beside you. Know which side you are on, and which bend means you have gone too far. A handrail is a landscape line. It is not a street. If it splits and you cannot say which branch, you stop.

If you are unsure, sit and sketch a circle of possible places instead of a hopeful dot. Signal from an open spot. Move only toward a feature you can name, in daylight, and camp if dusk is close. These notes do not replace a map you can read, and they do not invent a trail.
