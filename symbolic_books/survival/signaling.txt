Signaling: three of anything (whistle blasts, bright flashes, fires) is a common distress pattern. A mirror or light aimed at rescuers is a signal, not a weapon. Tell another person where you are going before you travel.

A whistle carries farther than a shout and spends less breath. Three short blasts, a pause, then three again is the pattern to repeat. If you hear a reply, answer the same way and stay where you can be seen. Yelling until you are hoarse does not mark your location.

Put bright gear in an open sight line, in a straight line or a large X. Flash a mirror toward a ridge or an aircraft in short groups of three, then stop so you do not dazzle someone close. Day smoke and a night flame are signals only where fire is legal and contained.

An arrow of stones or packs can show the way you left, if you must leave, and only where the sky can see it. Tell a person your route, your return time, and the color of your pack before you go. If you are unsure, signal and stay visible. This is how to be found, not a radio trick.
