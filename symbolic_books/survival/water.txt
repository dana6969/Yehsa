Water: carry known-safe water when you can. If you must treat water in the field, boiling is the straightforward heat method. Cloudy water should be settled or strained before boiling. This is an offline reminder, not a guarantee the water is safe, and not a substitute for emergency services.

Containers matter. A closed bottle or a pot you can heat beats a leaky bag. Keep the drinking bottle capped and off the ground, and refill it when you pass a source you mean to treat. A spare empty container is how you carry water away from the source.

Where to look: green growth on a dry slope, a dark seep, or a stream in the lowest line of a valley are clues. Flowing water is a better clue than a still pool. Clues are not proof. Settle the grit, then boil, and still treat the result as uncertain.

Snow and ice need melting before you drink them, and melting spends fuel you may need for warmth. Eating snow when you are already cold cools you further. Salt water and urine are not drinking water. This reminder sets no liter quota and no chemical dose.

Carry more than the first hour seems to need, then drink small amounts through the day instead of emptying the bottle at once. In heat, rest in shade before you feel dizzy. If someone stops sweating or grows confused, stop travel and get human help. This is not a medical threshold.
